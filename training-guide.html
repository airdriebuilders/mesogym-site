Training Guide

The ideas Meso Gym is built on, in plain English.

What Is RIR?

RIR means reps in reserve: how many more good reps you could have done when you stopped the set. Finishing a set at 2 RIR means you could have managed about two more with good form. A target like "4 sets × 8–12 reps @ 2 RIR" tells you how hard each set should feel, not just how many reps to do.

How A Mesocycle Works

A mesocycle (or "meso") is a training block, usually 4 to 6 working weeks followed by a deload week. Across the block:

  • Effort rises: the RIR target steps down week by week, so sets get closer to failure as the block goes on.
  • Volume ramps: sets per muscle start near your MEV (minimum effective volume, the least work that still drives progress) and build toward your MRV (maximum recoverable volume, the most you can recover from). The most taxing exercises, like heavy barbell lifts, start with fewer sets and lighter exercises take the rest.
  • Weights progress: each session suggests a weight based on what you did last time.

Deload Weeks And Why They Matter

Hard training builds fatigue faster than it builds fitness. A deload is a planned easier week, with fewer sets and lighter effort, that lets fatigue drop so you start the next block fresh. Deloads are part of the plan, not a sign you've failed.

Why Not Just Count Reps?

  • Effort control: RIR targets make sure sets are hard enough to count, without going to failure every time.
  • Progressive overload: suggested weights and a planned set ramp mean you do a little more over time, on purpose.
  • Managing fatigue: post-workout feedback and deloads stop volume from climbing past what you can recover from.

This guide is general training information, not medical advice. Check with a qualified professional if you have an injury or health condition.
